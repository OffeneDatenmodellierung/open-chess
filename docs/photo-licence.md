# The photo licence

*open-chess photo licence, version 1 (`open-chess-photo-licence-1`)*

When the board reader isn't sure of a photo you send, the server can keep
it so people can say what it shows. The board reader then learns from the
answers. It keeps your photos only if you accept this licence: in the
Chess lab (the first time a photo would be kept), or on the server's
Account page. You can change your answer there at any time.

## What you agree to

1. **You may share the photo.** You took it, or you have the right to send
   it. It shows a chess board, and nothing private: no faces you'd mind
   being seen, no documents or screens with personal details.
2. **The licence you give.** You give the operator of the server you send
   it to, and the open-chess project, a licence to the photo and to what
   people say it shows. The licence is:
   * worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive, perpetual and irrevocable;
   * to store, copy, crop, label and review the photo;
   * to use it to train, test and improve machine-learning models, of any
     kind and for any purpose, commercial or not, private or public;
   * to use, sell, publish and share those models and what they produce,
     without owing you anything.

   You keep your copyright in the photo.
3. **Who sees it.** People signed in to that server may see the photo, to
   say what it shows. Its operator and the open-chess project may share it
   with others who train models under these same terms.
4. **Your answers too.** What you say a photo shows, yours or someone
   else's, is covered by the same licence.

## Optional: the public dataset

You can also tick **Add my photos to the public dataset**. Then you
additionally dedicate the photos you send from then on to the public
domain, under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).
Anyone may then use them for anything, and they may be published in an
open dataset of chess board photos.

Leave it unticked and the photos are used only as described above: they
aren't published as photos.

## Changing your mind

Withdrawing on the Account page stops the server keeping your photos from
then on. You can also ask the server's operator to delete photos you sent.
They'll delete photos that haven't been used in training yet.

These can't be undone:
* models already trained on a photo;
* the licence for photos you sent before withdrawing;
* the public-domain dedication of photos you added to the public dataset.
